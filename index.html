Full Auditability & Provenance Ledger

Every bottling, producer, cask-link, mash-bill, tasting-note and price row in WhiskyDB carries a source ID (primary_source_id on spirits, source_id elsewhere) into the data_sources ledger, which records each source's name, endpoint URL, access timestamp and HTTP status; only the cask and flavor taxonomies, WhiskyDB's own vocabularies, carry none. § 04 · Frequently Asked Questions

Common inquiries regarding WhiskyDB normalization standards, schema, valuations, and licensing.

What data attributes are included in the WhiskyDB spirits database?

Each spirit record has a name, spirit type, ABV, bottle volume and a primary_source_id into the provenance ledger, and almost all carry a barcode or label ID and a distillery or producer link. Coverage is uneven, and stated plainly: 545 of the 2,721 spirits have an ABV other than 40.0; the rest show 40.0, either stated or the documented default for a source that states none. Bottle volume has documented defaults too: 750 mL on every US label-registry record, whose labels do not publish volume, and 700 mL on the curated seed rows and on Open Food Facts records without a stated quantity. Age statements are rare, and producer-published mash bills and flavor tags exist for only a handful of flagship bottlings.

How often are the secondary market auction valuations updated?

The auction data is WhiskyHunter's monthly mean winning bid per distillery: 7,231 monthly distillery auction benchmarks for 60 distilleries, from 2005 to 2024. They are distillery-level indices, never bottle prices; the data files carry them as 21,228 rows, one per linked bottling per month, and some bottlings linked by recent monthly refreshes carry only the latest months of their distillery's index. The monthly refresh re-checks the source for new months: the $49/month subscription delivers each refresh automatically, and the $49 one-time purchase delivers the current snapshot.

Can I download a free sample before purchasing a commercial license?

Yes! You can immediately download our public CSV sample datasets (spirits.csv and distilleries.csv) from our open-source GitHub repository or explore them interactively via our Hugging Face Sample Explorer without requiring an API key.

Where do WhiskyDB's mash bills and cask links come from?

Mash bills come only from producer-published specifications, and exist for a handful of bottlings. Cask links are either producer-published or derived by rule: a whole-word cask keyword in the bottling name (such as Sherry or Port), or the US legal requirement that bourbon and rye whiskey mature in new charred oak (27 CFR 5.143). Every bottling, producer, cask-link, mash-bill, tasting-note and price row carries a source ID into the data_sources provenance ledger (primary_source_id on spirits, source_id elsewhere). Apart from the documented ABV and bottle-volume defaults, an age statement, mash bill or cask link that no source states stays empty rather than guessed.
